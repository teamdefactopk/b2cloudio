The software behind a hosting company

Hosting companies run on a stack of connected systems: a website with domain search, a shopping cart, a billing and automation engine, a client dashboard, control panels that set up each service, and support tools such as tickets, live chat, a knowledge base and a status page. This page covers each system: the main options, 2026 pricing, what the market uses, and what fits b2cloud.io's five-country launch.

b2cloud.io strategy · build by default, buy where needed

Build vs buy plan

Build everything the customer sees and the business logic that sets b2cloud.io apart. Buy only where you'd otherwise rebuild a mature product, need a licence or accreditation, or need regulated infrastructure such as payments and tax.

Main risk: billing. Proration, upgrades and downgrades, failed-payment retries, credit notes and country-specific tax invoices for five entities are where home-built hosting platforms usually struggle. Build it on a double-entry ledger from day one, and let Stripe handle card storage, payment retries and tax calculation, so the PCI and tax burden stays with suppliers.

What the market uses

Build your own or assemble a stack

Large global hosts build their own

Hostinger (hPanel), GoDaddy, Namecheap, SiteGround (Site Tools), Kinsta (MyKinsta) and IONOS run their own dashboards, carts and control panels. This takes years of engineering, and the polished experience is part of what they sell.

Mid-size and regional hosts assemble one

Most mid-size hosts, including the Pakistani competitors reviewed earlier, run WHMCS (or Blesta, HostBill or Upmind) with cPanel or DirectAdmin, a theme such as Lagom, and add-on chat and helpdesk tools. That stack is fast to launch and proven, but looks generic unless you theme it.

The middle path

Launch on a proven billing engine, then build a custom b2cloud.io storefront and dashboard on top of its API. Upmind and HostBill are built API-first; WHMCS has an API and is widely used headless.

Pitfall: licence costs grow with you

WHMCS and cPanel both raised prices for 2026 and charge by client or account count. Choose panels with your margins in mind: DirectAdmin or Enhance cost far less per account than cPanel.

PHASE 1 · MONTHS 0–3

Launch

  • WHMCS + Lagom theme and one-step order form
  • DirectAdmin or cPanel shared servers
  • Openprovider domains + local registries
  • Stripe, PayPal, GoCardless
  • Crisp chat + WHMCS tickets
PHASE 2 · MONTHS 3–12

Scale support

  • Freshdesk or Zendesk with chat integrated
  • Status page + uptime monitoring
  • VirtFusion for VPS self-service
  • Microsoft CSP + Google reseller automation
  • Fraud screening + tax automation
PHASE 3 · YEAR 2+

Own the experience

  • Custom storefront and dashboard on the billing API
  • Mobile app for account management
  • AI support assistant on the knowledge base
  • Partner and reseller portal
  • Re-evaluate billing (HostBill, Upmind or custom)
